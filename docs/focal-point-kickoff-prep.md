# Focal Point kickoff prep

Pre-call brief for the Focal Point discovery/kickoff conversation.

**Stakes:** Focal Point is the realistic candidate for customer #1. Customer #1
unlocks every downstream piece: social proof for the cold campaign, a real
testimonial for the deck, calibration data for the scoring function, voice
samples for the drafter, every reference to "a church using Grace today" in
future outreach.

This call matters more than any other action this month.

---

## 1. Pre-call research (do this 24 hours before)

Spend 30 minutes learning the actual ministry. Pastors can tell within 90
seconds whether you've done the homework. The questions you ask later
should reflect things you already know.

**Website pass — open their site and find:**

- [ ] Senior pastor's name + tenure (if visible)
- [ ] Associate or executive pastor's name
- [ ] Office admin or executive assistant (look on staff page)
- [ ] Service times (Sunday morning, midweek)
- [ ] Denominational affiliation or "non-denominational"
- [ ] Whether they have an "I'm new" / "First-time visitor" page
- [ ] Whether they have a connect card form online
- [ ] Sermon archive freshness (last sermon date)
- [ ] Live stream presence
- [ ] Visible ChMS footer badges (Planning Center, Tithe.ly, Pushpay, Breeze, etc.)
- [ ] Social media handles + last post date

**Search pass:**

- [ ] Google reviews — count + average + scan top 3 negative reviews if any
- [ ] Facebook page — recent activity (last 30 days?)
- [ ] YouTube — sermon view counts (rough attendance proxy)
- [ ] Any news / press mentions in the last 12 months

**Estimate weekly attendance.** Methods that work:
- YouTube sermon view counts × 1.5 (online viewers tend to be 50–80% of in-person)
- Google reviews count is roughly proportional to size (rough: 1 review per 10–20 weekly attendees over the church's lifetime)
- "About us" page may state it
- Look for staff page size — 1 paid pastor = under 100; 3–5 paid staff = 200–500; 6–10 paid staff = 500–1,500

Write down your estimate before the call. Verify on the call.

**Anti-research:** Don't make assumptions about theology, politics, or
denomination beyond what's stated. Pastors are sensitive to being
stereotyped. Ask, don't assume.

---

## 2. Pre-call mental setup

Before the call, write down on a sticky note:

> "This is a conversation. Not a pitch. If I push too hard,
> they say no. If I'm a real human, they pilot."

Three reminders for yourself:

1. **You're the founder. They're talking to the founder.** That's already
   the social proof in this call. Don't apologize for being early-stage.
2. **You've served in church ministry for 20 years.** That credibility is
   real. Lean on it in moments of objection.
3. **You don't need this customer.** You'd like to have them. The desperate
   energy will close them out. The confident energy gets them excited.

---

## 3. Call opening (first 5 minutes)

**Opening line (after pleasantries):**

> "Pastor [Name], I'm going to do something different than most demos.
> Before I show you anything, I want to understand how [Church name] does
> ministry. Most of what I'd show you needs to be calibrated to your
> reality. If I just demo the generic version, neither of us learns what
> matters. So I'd love to ask you some questions for the first 15 minutes.
> Then I'll show you Grace. Then we can talk about whether this is a fit
> for [Church name]. Sound okay?"

**Why this works:**
- It signals respect (asking before showing)
- It earns the right to demo by listening first
- It frames the call as a real conversation
- It separates demo from pitch (lowers their guard)

---

## 4. Discovery questions (15 minutes)

Pick 5–7 of these. Don't run through all of them. Listen for the wedge.

### About them and the church

1. "How long have you been at [Church name]?"
2. "How would you describe the church to someone who's never visited?"
3. "What's your size? In-person attendance Sunday morning?"
4. "Who else is on staff? Paid, part-time, volunteer?"
5. "Are you involved in week-to-week operations or do you have an admin who handles that?"

### About their visitor reality (the wedge for Grace)

1. "Walk me through what happens when a first-time family fills out a connect card. From the moment they hand it in to whoever does the follow-up."
2. "How long does it typically take to get back to a first-time visitor?"
3. "Do you have a system for that, or is it more 'whoever-remembers'?"
4. "When was the last time you found out a visitor came back vs. didn't?"
5. "Be honest: of every 10 first-time families, how many do you actually see again?"

**Listen for:** Numbers like "I don't know," "maybe half," "less than I'd like." Those are the moments to lean in. Don't fix them yet. Note them. They'll come back into the demo.

### About their members (the second wedge)

1. "How do you find out when a member is drifting? Three Sundays missed, four Sundays missed?"
2. "When someone leaves the church, when do you typically find out? Day they leave, or six weeks later?"
3. "Care needs — health, grief, job loss — how do those usually surface to you?"

### About their tools

1. "What are you using for member management? Planning Center? Breeze?"
2. "How are you handling weekly communications? Email tool? Texts?"
3. "What's the biggest pain point in your current stack?"

### About their season

1. "What are the 2 or 3 things you're most focused on as a church this year?"
2. "Anything you'd like to be doing that you're not doing because there's no time?"
3. "Is anything on the board's plate right now around AI or tech?"

## 5. Demo (15 minutes)

**Demo principle: show their reality, not your features.**

The dashboard they'll see is at `/dashboard/focal-point-test` — same module
set as Cornerstone. The fixture data is generic. Your job is to map every
screen back to what they just told you.

### Demo flow

1. **Today page (3 minutes).** "This is what your Monday morning looks like. Here's the brief Grace writes you Sunday night. Here's the sign-off queue with drafts waiting for your team. Here's the visitor list from Sunday — every one has a drafted message ready to review."

2. **Front Desk / Guests (3 minutes).** "Walk through one visitor with me. Sunday's connect card. Grace pulls the name, reads the context, drafts a personal text in your pastor's voice. You read it. You hit send. Visitor sees a text from their pastor's number by Tuesday."

3. **Care & Drift (3 minutes).** "Here's where members appear when they've missed three Sundays. Grace surfaces them BEFORE they're gone. Click any name to see drafted check-in text. You can send, you can change the cadence, you can mark 'I've got this.'"

4. **Settings/integrations (2 minutes — only if they ask).** "Here's how Grace connects to Planning Center. One auth flow. No data migration. She reads what's there. She doesn't replace it."

5. **The math slide (2 minutes).** Pull up slide 4 of the deck. Walk through the 80% → 50–70% conversion math. Anchor on their numbers: "If you're seeing 8 first-time families a month and converting 1 right now, this gets you to 4–5. That's 3 to 4 additional families per month. Over 12 months that's 40+ families that stayed because of one system."

6. **Stop and ask.** "What questions are coming up for you?"

## 6. Objection handling

Pre-loaded responses for the most likely pushback. Use these as starting
points, not scripts.

### "This feels impersonal."

> "I hear that one a lot. Here's the honest answer: every message Grace
> drafts is reviewed by someone on your team before it sends. The voice
> is your pastor's voice, calibrated from samples we pull during setup.
> Visitors never know AI was involved. The alternative — which is what
> most churches have today — is that visitors get nothing personal at all.
> That's the actual impersonal experience."

### "We already use Planning Center."

> "Good. Planning Center is the database. Grace is the layer that makes
> the database do something on your behalf. Planning Center stores your
> first-timer. Grace drafts the follow-up, surfaces the drift, writes the
> weekly brief. They work together, not against each other."

### "What does this cost?"

> "We'll go through the specific number for [Church name] in a moment.
> Here's the frame: less than a part-time admin hire. For our first
> 10 churches, we're offering founding partner pricing that locks in for
> 2 years. Want me to walk through the tiers?"

(Have the actual tier ranges ready: small / mid / large. Don't quote a
number on the call without context — quote with their attendance band.
Reference: ~$599/mo small, ~$899/mo mid, ~$1,299/mo large as your internal
framing. Adjust as needed. **Get explicit sign-off before sharing pricing.**)

### "I need to bring this to my board."

> "Of course. That's the right way to do this. Here's what I can do
> for you. I'll prep a one-page brief specifically for your board meeting
> with the investment, the expected outcomes, what data Grace sees, and a
> clear rollback plan. You'll have it within 48 hours. When's the board
> meeting?"

### "What if it doesn't work?"

> "Month-to-month. No annual contract. If it's not delivering value after
> 60 days, we'll work with you on a clean rollback. Your data exports out.
> We don't hold ChMS data hostage."

### "We don't have an AI policy."

> "73% of churches don't yet. We've drafted a starter policy specifically
> for how Grace gets used in a church context. I'll send you a copy. It's
> part of onboarding, not a prerequisite."

### "What about privacy?"

> "Grace only sees what you give her access to. No data is shared with
> other churches. No data is used to train external models. Full export
> on request, anytime. SOC 2 controls in progress and I can walk you
> through the specifics if useful."

### "We're not sure now is the right time."

> "Totally fair. What would make it the right time? Is it a budget cycle?
> Is it a leadership conversation? Is it just bandwidth right now?"

(Listen. Then offer the specific easier next step that matches what they
said. A board brief if it's leadership. A two-month delay if it's budget.
A simpler pilot scope if it's bandwidth.)

---

## 7. Close framework

After demo + objections, ask the close question:

> "Pastor [Name], based on what you've seen, here's what I'm hearing.
> [Summarize their wedge — visitor follow-up, member drift, whatever
> they leaned into]. Does that match what you'd want this to actually
> deliver at [Church name]?"

Wait for them to say yes (or refine).

Then:

> "What I'd love to do is run a 60-day pilot. We'd get you set up over
> the next week and have you live before [date — pick a near Sunday]. At
> the 30-day check-in we look at what's working. At 60 days we decide
> whether this graduates to a regular partnership. What would you need
> from me to make that happen?"

**Their answer determines the next step:**

- "Yes, let's do it" → confirm decision-maker chain, send contract within 24 hours
- "I need to talk to my board/wife/admin" → schedule the follow-up call, prep the board brief
- "I'm not ready" → diagnose the real blocker, schedule a checkin 4 weeks out
- "It's not a fit" → ask why, listen, offer to introduce them to another tool if appropriate

---

## 8. Within 24 hours of the call

Regardless of outcome:

- [ ] Send personalized thank-you email recapping what you discussed
- [ ] If they asked for the board brief, send the board brief
- [ ] If they're "thinking about it," send the deck PDF + one-paragraph framing
- [ ] If they're "ready," send contract + setup intake form
- [ ] Update CRM with notes, next step, decision-maker chain, expected close date
- [ ] Set yourself a calendar reminder for the agreed-upon followup date

## 9. The board brief template (if they ask)

Keep this to one page. Boards skim.

```
TO:        [Church name] Board / Elders
FROM:      Pastor [Name]
RE:        Bringing Grace (CommandSite) on as the church's ministry assistant
DATE:      [date]

The opportunity
80% of first-time visitors at small-to-mid churches never return. The #1
reason cited isn't the sermon or the music. It's that nobody followed up.
Churches with a documented 48-hour follow-up system convert visitors at
50–70%, vs. the 10–20% national baseline. (Source: VisiConnect; The
Effective Church Group.)

What Grace does
Grace is an AI ministry assistant who watches our Planning Center
first-timer list every Sunday, drafts personal pastoral follow-ups within
2 hours in our pastor's voice, and queues them for our team to review and
send. She also surfaces members who have missed 3+ Sundays before they're
gone for good, drafts the weekly Sunday email, and writes our Monday
morning brief.

What Grace is not
She is not a replacement for our pastoral team. Every message that leaves
the church is approved by a human on staff before it sends. She is not
an autonomous AI. She is not a chat bot that interacts with our members.

The investment
$[X] per month. Less than the cost of a part-time admin hire. Month-to-
month, no annual contract. We've negotiated founding partner pricing
locked in for 2 years.

Expected outcomes (90 days)
- First-time visitor return rate moves from ~[current]% toward 50–70%
- Member drift identified within 3 weeks, not 6
- Staff hours saved per week: ~10

Rollback
60-day pilot. Cancel any time, clean data export. If we decide Grace is
not delivering value, we walk away.

Data and privacy
Grace sees only what we give her access to. No data is shared with other
churches or used to train external models. SOC 2 controls in progress.
Starter AI use policy template provided as part of onboarding.

Recommendation
Approve a 60-day pilot starting [date]. Decision required by [date].
```

## 10. After the call, regardless of outcome

The single most important thing: **write down what surprised you.**

- What did the pastor lean into that you didn't expect?
- What objection came up that wasn't on your list?
- What did the deck or demo land flat on?
- What language did the pastor use that you should steal?

This is the calibration data. It feeds everything else. The cold-email
sequence we build next is shaped by what you hear in this conversation.

If Focal Point converts, this is customer #1.
If Focal Point doesn't convert, this is the most valuable piece of
research you'll do this quarter.

Either way, you win.
