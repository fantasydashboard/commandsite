## Section 3 — What Grace Actually Does

**Section heading:** Five things Grace handles for your team.

Five module cards in a 2x3 grid (or vertical stack on mobile). Each card: icon + title + 1-line + 1 sentence of detail.

### 📞 Grace at the front desk
**Catches every call, every form, every connect card.**
Trained on your ministries, service times, and team. Routes pastoral emergencies straight to your cell, captures first-time visitor info, and handles general questions warmly — 24/7. Sounds like a thoughtful volunteer, not a chatbot.

### 👋 Grace welcomes every first-timer
**Within hours, not weeks.**
Grace drafts a personal text or email in your pastor's voice within 2 hours of a Sunday visit. Day 3, day 7, day 14, day 30 — gentle, mission-aligned next-step invitations to small groups, kids' programs, or a coffee with the pastor. Always personal, never spammy.

### 🌱 Grace captures the stories
**The testimonies you never have time to gather.**
After a baptism, a small group milestone, or a notable life moment, Grace asks for the story (with full permission) and drafts share-ready quotes for Sunday slides, your website, and social. Your church is starved for stories — this is how you start collecting them.

### 🔁 Grace notices when someone drifts
**Before they're gone for good.**
Members who haven't attended in 60-90 days get a personal "we miss you, can we pray for anything?" outreach. Drafts always go through your pastoral team for review before sending — never fully automated for pastoral care.

### 📊 Grace's weekly report
**One screen. The numbers your team actually cares about.**
Weekly attendance trends, first-time visitors, follow-up completion rates, dormant members reached, prayer requests received, volunteer pipeline. Open it Monday morning over coffee and see what the week ahead actually looks like.

**Plus — Ask Grace anything.** A chat box right on the dashboard: *"Grace, did the Hendricks family visit last week?" "Grace, who haven't I prayed for this week?" "Grace, what came in over the weekend?"* She knows your congregation — ask her like she's the church admin who has every detail at her fingertips.

## Section 4 — Why "Custom-Built" Matters for Churches

**Section heading:** Built for your church, not the church a software company imagined.

Most church tech ships a generic toolkit and assumes every church works the same. CommandSite gets built around how *your* church actually does ministry. Three steps:

### 1. We learn your church
A discovery call where we map your services, your ministries, your follow-up rhythms, and how your team actually communicates. We learn your pastor's voice so the AI's drafts sound like you, not like a SaaS template.

### 2. We build your system
We train your AI on what we learned, set up your integrations (Planning Center, your ChMS, your giving platform), and run test scenarios until everything sounds pastoral. Live in 14 days.

### 3. We tune as you grow
Monthly check-ins to refine what's working. Seasonal adjustments (the way Christmas hits is different from regular Sunday rhythm). As your church grows, the system grows with you.

## Section 5 — On the "AI Feels Impersonal" Question

**Section heading:** Some pastors hear "AI" and stop listening. We get it.

Grace isn't a chatbot pretending to be human. She's a ministry assistant who handles the parts of church admin that are already broken or missed entirely.

- **For pastoral care, Grace never replaces a human.** She drafts, she queues, she routes — but a real person on your team reads, edits, and sends. Your team stays in the relationship.
- **For systems work — first-time visitor texts, dormant-member check-ins, story collection — Grace just handles it.** These are the touches that already aren't happening because nobody has time. The choice isn't "AI does it vs. a human does it." It's "Grace does it vs. nobody does it."
- **You set the line.** Some churches want every outbound message reviewed. Others want only the pastoral ones reviewed. Grace works around your team's comfort level.

The result: more first-time families followed up with. More dormant members reached. More stories captured. Your pastor gets their evenings back.

## Section 8 — FAQ

**Section heading:** Common questions

Six FAQs, accordion-style.

**Q: Won't the AI make our church feel impersonal?**
The opposite, actually. The AI handles the systems work that's currently *not happening* — the first-time visitor texts that nobody has time to send, the dormant-member check-ins that fall through the cracks, the prayer requests that sit in voicemail. Your pastoral team stays in the relationships. The AI just makes sure no one falls through the cracks before someone reaches out.

**Q: We already use Planning Center / ChurchTeams / Breeze. Does this replace it?**
No. CommandSite plugs into your existing ChMS. We're the missing layer between your visitors and your tools — the part that catches the connection, drafts the follow-up, and notifies the right person on your team. Your existing system stays.

**Q: What about pastoral confidentiality? Where does our data live?**
Your data lives in a dedicated, isolated environment — not shared with other churches. Pastoral notes, prayer requests, and member data are encrypted at rest and in transit. We never share or sell anything, and we follow the same security standards as any reputable church management platform. You own everything.

**Q: We're a small church. Is this overkill for us?**
Our Small Church tier is built for exactly that range — under 250 attenders, one staff admin, can't justify hiring a full-time church administrator but can't keep losing first-time families either. If that's you, this is for you.

**Q: How long does setup actually take?**
14 days from kickoff to live. We do the heavy lifting — your team's only "homework" is a discovery call, an intro to your existing tools, and a final review before we go live. Most pastors are surprised at how little of their time it takes.

**Q: Our elder board / finance committee will need to review this. Is there a way to share?**
Yes — after the discovery walkthrough, we'll send you a one-page summary you can share with your leadership team or finance committee. Annual pricing is available for churches who prefer to budget once a year.

**Q: Why is she called Grace?**
Because grace — unearned help, quiet support — is exactly what we want her to embody for your church. Not a flashy tool. Not a replacement for ministry. Just a quiet, faithful presence that handles the systems work so your team can do the people work.

**Q: Does Grace identify herself as AI when she answers calls?**
Yes — Grace always introduces herself as your AI assistant on the first interaction. We think transparency is the right move pastorally and it's becoming legally required in some states. Most callers are pleasantly surprised at how naturally she handles the conversation once they know.

- `commandsite.io/for-churches` (more verbose but slightly clearer intent)

For tonight: **`commandsite.io/churches`** is simplest. Single Framer project with two pages, same DNS.

## Page parity with the local-services page

These two pages should feel like siblings — same brand, same visual language, but different audience. Reuse:
- Same logo + nav header
- Same color palette (CommandSite brand colors)
- Same typography
- Same Calendly integration

Differ:
- Hero image (church dashboard vs. service business dashboard)
- Module cards (Front Desk vs. AI Receptionist, etc.)
- Tone (warmer / pastoral on this page; founder-direct on the other)

## Pre-launch checklist (church-specific)

- [ ] Confirm pricing numbers — especially annual discount math
- [ ] Verify all uses of "AI" feel non-threatening (the FAQ + Section 5 should defuse most of it)
- [ ] Get a second pastor/church admin's eyes on the copy if possible — they'll catch tone-deaf phrasing you and I will miss
- [ ] Make sure the Calendly event description specifically says "for church leaders" so it doesn't surface a generic SaaS sales call vibe
- [ ] Add a "Schedule for after Sunday" disclaimer somewhere — pastors are slammed Sundays + Mondays. Smart Calendly availability matters.

## When to push this page

Don't blast it cold. The church page exists primarily to:
1. **Send to the church that's already interested** (the unlock that prompted this build)
2. **Send to warm intros** from your kids ministry network
3. **Show as a credibility signal** when a pastor finds CommandSite via the main page footer ("oh, they do churches too")

Cold-emailing churches works very differently from cold-emailing service businesses (slower, more relationship-driven, more deliverability-sensitive). Don't mix the two channels — when you're ready to actively pursue churches at scale, we'll build a separate outbound playbook.
