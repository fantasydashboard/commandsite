# Warm-pastor outreach starting drafts

Hand-crafted starting points for the 5–10 pastors Josh already knows.
These are NOT cold-email templates. They lean on existing relationship and
Josh's ministry background. Edit liberally; the goal is to get a real
conversation started and learn what pastors actually respond to.

## How to use these

1. Pick 5–10 pastors you have a real relationship with (former coworkers,
   conference contacts, family connections, people you've served alongside).
2. For each one, pick the template that fits the relationship type closest.
3. Rewrite every email in your own words. Don't send any of these verbatim.
   The point is to get real reply data on framing that's actually you.
4. Send Tuesday–Thursday morning (pastor inbox time). Avoid Sundays, Mondays,
   and Saturday afternoons.
5. Track replies in a spreadsheet so we learn what's actually working before
   we start automating any of it.

The four research stats you can reach for in any of these:

- **80% of first-time visitors never return.** Source: VisiConnect, 2024
- **#1 cited reason isn't the sermon. It's no follow-up.** Source: The Effective Church Group
- **Churches with 48-hour follow-up systems convert 50–70%** vs. 10–20% baseline
- **That's a 4–5x improvement** on people who already wanted to come back

## Template 1 — For a pastor you've served with directly

> Subject: Quick question about how y'all handle visitor follow-up
>
> Hey [Name],
>
> Random one. Been working on something I'd love your gut reaction on, since you're one of the few pastors I know who actually thinks systemically about this stuff.
>
> Here's the question I keep wrestling with: every pastor I've ever served alongside knew that 80% of first-time visitors never come back, and that the #1 reason isn't the sermon, it's that nobody followed up. And every one of them genuinely cared. They just didn't have a system that ran on its own.
>
> So I built one. It's called Grace. She's an AI ministry assistant who watches your Sunday connect cards and drafts personal, pastoral follow-ups in your voice, queued for your team to review and send. The drafts go out within 48 hours. The churches piloting this are seeing 4–5x returns on first-time visitors.
>
> I'm not pitching you. I'm asking: if you were thinking about a system like this for [Church name], what would you want to see? What would make you not want it? I trust your gut on this stuff more than most.
>
> Would love 20 minutes on the phone whenever you have it.
>
> Josh

**Why this works:** Treats them as advisor, not prospect. The "what would make you not want it" question shows you're not selling. They'll either book the call or write back with the objection that needs solving.

## Template 2 — For a pastor at a church that fits the ICP (200–800)

> Subject: First-time visitors at [Church name]
>
> Hey [Name],
>
> Hope ministry's good. I keep thinking about the conversation we had at [event/last time you saw them] about first-time visitors.
>
> Here's what I've been building. Something specifically for the small-to-mid-church reality: you've got 5–15 connect cards on a Sunday, the pastor's day off is Monday, and by Friday nobody has touched the stack. Then those families are at the megachurch down the road that texted them the same afternoon.
>
> The thing I built is called Grace. She drafts a personal text or email to each first-time visitor within 2 hours of service, in your pastor's voice. Your team reviews and sends. The research says this turns the visitor return rate from 10–20% into 50–70%.
>
> Curious if it'd be useful to walk through what it'd look like at [Church name]. 30 minutes if you have it. No pressure.
>
> Josh

**Why this works:** Specific to their reality. The Monday-day-off line tells them you understand pastoral rhythm. Closes with a low-commitment ask.

## Template 4 — For a pastor you respect but barely know

> Subject: Permission to ask a question?
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> We've met a few times, most recently [event/context]. I've followed [Church name]'s ministry from a distance and have a lot of respect for the way you've led through [specific thing].
>
> I'm in an early stage of building something for small-to-mid churches and I'm trying to talk to 10 pastors before I assume I know what they need. You're on the short list.
>
> Would you give me 20 minutes? I'd ask you about how [Church name] handles visitor follow-up and member drift, and tell you what I'm building. You can push back hard, I'd actually prefer it.
>
> No selling. Just learning.
>
> Josh

**Why this works:** Permission-based opener respects the relationship distance. "Just learning" sets a low-stakes frame. Many pastors will take this call as a favor.

## What to track

For each email sent, log:

| Field | Values |
| --- | --- |
| Pastor name | |
| Church name | |
| Relationship strength | strong / medium / acquaintance |
| Template used | 1–10 above |
| Sent date | Tuesday/Wednesday/Thursday recommended |
| Touch 1 reply? | yes / no |
| Touch 2 sent? Reply? | |
| Touch 3 sent? Reply? | |
| Booked call? | |
| Outcome | pilot / "let me think" / no / referral |

Goal: after 10 emails, you should know:
- Which template structure pulls replies (relationship language? stat-led? short?)
- What pastors push back on (price concern? AI concern? time concern?)
- What objections to write into the cold-email sequence later

This is what unlocks the cold campaign. We don't write Touch 1 for strangers
until Touch 1 for friends has been tested.

## What NOT to do

- Don't send these in batches. One per day, max two. They should read as
  personal because they are.
- Don't follow up more than 3 times. Past day 14 with no reply, drop it.
  These are friends and contacts.
- Don't pitch pricing in the cold touch. Pastors decide slow and with a
  board; pricing belongs in the discovery call.
- Don't reference the deck on Touch 1. The deck is for the call, not the
  inbox.
- Don't sound like a marketer. If a sentence reads like a sales email,
  rewrite it.
